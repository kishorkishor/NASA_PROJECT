Burn Before the Rain

Every spring the Chittagong hills burn; every monsoon the hills slide. We fixed a satellite record that says burning tripled (it didn't), then listed the steep, populated places where fire was detected, each with its corrected 24-season history, so they can be checked before the rain.

Fire data: NASA FIRMS (MODIS Aqua, VIIRS S-NPP), 2003 to 30 June 2026.

The false alarm

NASA's old fire satellite (MODIS, 1 km pixels) was joined in 2012 by a sharper one (VIIRS, 375 m). VIIRS sees one fire as several dots and catches small fires MODIS misses. Join the two archives as they are and Bangladesh seems to burn far more after 2012.

The corrected record

We shrink VIIRS to MODIS-sized ~1 km footprints, then learn how many VIIRS fire-days equal one MODIS detection in each region and season, using only the years when both satellites flew and MODIS was reliable. The jump disappears: under this calibration, detected fire activity in Bangladesh has gone down.

Inspect a place

Each square is about 2 km. We score where fire was detected on steep ground with people nearby, measured at the fire points themselves, then mark as "inspect first" only places that stay in the top 50 in at least 80% of 54 settings of our own scoring grid. It is a list of places to check before June, not a landslide forecast.

Limits we know about

  • Fire detected is not a bare slope. Satellites record heat, not burned area. Our Sentinel-2 test is spectral evidence consistent with burning for a sample of 60 places. It is not a visual check; a blind visual check is prepared but not yet done.
  • No landslide records were checked. Burned steep slopes are a warning sign, not a proven cause. Many deaths happen where hills are cut for houses, which fire satellites cannot see.
  • MODIS is a reference, not the truth. It also drifted in orbit from 2024 and had an outage in April 2022; both are excluded from calibration.
  • The weights are ours. The ranking changes with thresholds. "Inspect first" means stable within our own grid of settings, not a probability of being right.
  • Jhum is a traditional livelihood. This list is for support and safety checks with communities, not for blame.
  • Not yet reviewed by people who work in the hill districts.
